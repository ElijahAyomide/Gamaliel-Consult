Dispute resolution

Mediation: making room for a different conversation

10 September 2026 · 6 min read · By Gamaliel Consult

Mediation gives people in a dispute the chance to talk through the issues with the help of a neutral person, often resolving matters faster and at lower cost than formal proceedings. Preparation makes the difference.

What mediation is

A mediator does not decide who is right. Their role is to help each side understand the other’s position and explore options for settlement. The process is usually voluntary and confidential, and discussions are generally held without prejudice, meaning they cannot be used later as an admission.

Preparing well

Be clear about what you want to achieve and what you would accept. Gather the key documents, understand the strengths and weaknesses of your position, and make sure that whoever attends has authority to agree a settlement on the day.

During the session

Expect an opening discussion followed by private conversations between the mediator and each side. Staying open, listening and being willing to consider creative solutions often leads to better outcomes than holding firmly to a fixed position.

If you reach agreement

Agreements are normally recorded in writing and, once signed, can be legally binding. It is worth having the terms checked so that they are clear and workable.

When it may not suit

Mediation is not right for every dispute. Where urgent action is needed, or where one side is unwilling to take part in good faith, other steps may be more appropriate.

This article provides general information only. The law and its application depend on the facts and jurisdiction. Please seek advice about your own circumstances.
