A considered review today can help prevent uncertainty tomorrow. Most contract problems do not come from dramatic disagreements. They come from details that were never discussed, or assumed to mean different things to each side. These are the questions worth asking before you commit.

## 1. Are the right parties named?

Check that the names, legal status and addresses are correct, and that the person signing has the authority to bind the business. If you are dealing with a company, it matters whether the contract is with the company itself, an affiliate or an individual.

## 2. Is the scope clear?

Ask what exactly is being provided, by when and to what standard. Vague descriptions leave room for different expectations. If something matters to you, such as a deadline, a deliverable or a service level, it should be written down.

## 3. How and when does payment work?

Look at the price, what it includes, when invoices are issued and how long you have to pay. Check for charges that can be added later, such as interest, expenses or increases, and what happens if there is a genuine dispute about an invoice.

## 4. How long does it last, and how does it end?

Note the start date, the length of the term and whether the contract renews automatically. Then read the termination clause closely. Can either side end it early, on what notice, and what happens to work already done or money already paid?

> “The best way forward starts with understanding what matters most.”

## 5. Who carries the risk?

Liability clauses decide who bears the cost if something goes wrong. Look for limits on what can be claimed, exclusions for certain kinds of loss, and any requirement to hold insurance. Terms that look routine can shift a great deal of risk to one side.

## 6. What happens if there is a disagreement?

Check which law governs the contract, where disputes will be heard and whether there are steps to follow first, such as discussion or mediation. A practical process written in advance can save time and cost later.

## 7. Does it reflect what was agreed?

Many contracts state that they replace everything discussed before. If a promise was made in an email or a meeting and it matters to you, make sure it appears in the final document.

## Before you sign

Read the whole document, not just the summary or the pages you are asked to sign. Ask for anything you do not understand to be explained in plain language. If the contract is important to your business or your future, it is worth having it reviewed before you commit.
