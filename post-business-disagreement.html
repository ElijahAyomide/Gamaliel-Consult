Dispute resolution

When a business disagreement becomes a dispute

15 October 2026 · 5 min read · By Gamaliel Consult

Most business disagreements start small: a late payment, a missed deadline, a difference in how a clause is read. Knowing when a disagreement is turning into a dispute, and responding in a measured way, can protect both your position and the relationship.

Recognising the warning signs

Communication becomes slower or more formal. Positions harden on both sides. Lawyers or formal notices appear. Payments are withheld, or one party starts to rely on the strict wording of the contract rather than what was intended. Any of these is a good moment to pause and take stock.

Start by understanding your position

Gather the contract and any related documents, emails and invoices, and write a simple timeline of what happened and when. Be clear about what outcome you actually want, whether that is payment, performance, a changed arrangement or a clean exit. A clear objective makes every later decision easier.

Consider the options before escalating

A direct conversation or a without-prejudice negotiation is often the quickest and least costly route. Mediation brings in a neutral person to help both sides find common ground. Formal proceedings may be necessary, but they take time, cost money and can damage relationships, so they are best considered alongside the alternatives.

Mind the time limits and notice requirements

Claims can be lost if they are not brought within a set period, and many contracts require notice to be given in a particular way before certain steps are taken. Check these early so that you do not limit your own options.

A proportionate way forward

The right approach depends on what is at stake, the strength of your position and what you want your business relationships to look like afterwards. Early, practical advice usually leaves you with more choices.

This article provides general information only. The law and its application depend on the facts and jurisdiction. Please seek advice about your own circumstances.
