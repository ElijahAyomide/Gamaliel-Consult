Regulatory & compliance

Building compliance into everyday business

17 September 2026 · 4 min read · By Gamaliel Consult

Compliance works best when it is part of how a business operates, not a task completed once a year. A thoughtful, proportionate approach helps you spot risk early and respond with confidence.

Know which obligations apply to you

Identify the laws, regulations and contractual commitments that affect your sector and activities. Keep a simple record of what applies, who is responsible and when it needs to be reviewed.

Give responsibility a clear owner

Someone should be accountable for each area, with enough time and authority to do the job. In smaller businesses this may be one person with a wide remit, which makes clear priorities all the more important.

Keep policies practical

Policies are only useful if people understand and follow them. Write them in plain language, keep them short and make sure they reflect what actually happens in the business.

Train, record and review

Regular, relevant training and good records help show that you take your obligations seriously. Review how things are working, learn from incidents and near misses, and update your approach as the business and the rules change.

Scale it to your size

Proportionality matters. A small business does not need the systems of a large organisation, but it does need to understand its risks and manage them sensibly.

This article provides general information only. The law and its application depend on the facts and jurisdiction. Please seek advice about your own circumstances.
